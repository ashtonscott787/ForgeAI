An AI employee
that lives in your building.

Most AI is a website you rent. ForgeAI is a program you install. It runs on the computers you already own, works with your real email, files and customers, and does whole jobs rather than handing back paragraphs.

In one paragraph

What it actually is.

ForgeAI is a desktop app for Mac and Windows, and a headless version for a Linux box. Inside it there is a chat, your mailbox, your files, a place for the websites it builds, and a set of agents that work on a schedule. Behind all of that sits a model — either a free one running on your own machine, or Claude or ChatGPT if you paste in a key. You decide which, per job, and it tells you which one answered.

Because it runs locally, it can see things a website never can: the actual spreadsheet on your disk, the mailbox with the real orders in it, the computers in the next room. And because it runs locally, nothing has to leave the building for it to work.

Ways to run it

Five setups, same app.

Start with the first one. You can move up at any time without reinstalling anything or losing your work.

Setup 1

Just your Mac

Install it, get a free model, use it like any other app. Everything lives on that one machine. This is where almost everyone starts, and plenty of people never leave.

Setup 2

Your Mac, used from the couch

Turn on remote access and the same ForgeAI opens in a browser on your phone, your iPad or the laptop downstairs. The work still happens on the Mac; the other devices are just windows onto it.

Setup 3

A few computers, joined

Join the office PC and the machine in the back room with a four-digit code. They pool their models and memory, and each job goes to whichever computer will finish it first — so the PC with the graphics card quietly does the heavy lifting.

Setup 4

One always-on server

Put the headless Linux edition on a box in the cupboard. It answers the phone, runs the agents overnight and serves everyone in the building, whether or not anyone's laptop is open.

Setup 5

More than one site

Two premises, linked privately over the network with their own encrypted connection. Each site keeps its own data and its own people; shared work crosses over only where you allow it, and both sides log it.

All five are the same download. Nothing is a different edition.
