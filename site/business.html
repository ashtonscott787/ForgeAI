One laptop today.
A whole company later.

The same download runs a one-person trade business and a company across two sites. Nothing is a different edition, nothing needs reinstalling, and the bill does not grow with your headcount.

Day one

It starts as an app on your Mac.

Free, no account, no card. One person doing their own admin, with a model running on the machine in front of them.

When you outgrow it

The other computers join in.

Four digits on the second machine and they are working together — sharing models and memory, with each job going to whichever will finish it first.

Then the staff

Everyone in the building, from their own screen.

Sign-in codes rather than invented passwords. You decide what each person can see and do, and every action is logged.

Then the second site

Two premises, one private link.

Each site keeps its own data and its own people. Work crosses between them only where you allow it, and both ends keep a record.

What it never becomes

A bill that grows with the payroll.

Twelve people on four computers across two sites, and still no monthly per-seat charge — because the work is happening on hardware you already bought.

By size

What it actually does at each stage.

The same features, used very differently depending on how many of you there are.

Just you

The job is getting the admin off your evenings. One computer, the free plan, done.

  • Quotes and invoices from your own price list
  • Inbox sorted and the easy replies drafted overnight
  • A website with a booking form, built in an afternoon
  • Receipts and paperwork read and filed

Plan: Personal — free

Two to ten people

Now customers are involved, and so are staff who need the same answers you would give.

  • The phone line and website chat answering for you
  • Shared knowledge so everyone quotes the same prices
  • Agents doing the Friday reports and the invoice chasing
  • The back-room PC joined in to take the heavy jobs

Plan: Business — one-off per computer

Ten to a hundred

Departments with different work, different confidentiality, and someone who will ask about the audit trail.

  • Separate organisations, each with their own data and people
  • Permissions per person; approvals before anything is sent or spent
  • A model trained on how your company actually writes
  • A server in the cupboard doing the overnight work

Plan: Business or Site licence

More than one site

Two addresses, or a head office and a shop. Each wants its own data; some work has to cross.

  • Sites linked privately, each keeping its own information
  • Cross-site work off by default, allowed rule by rule, logged on both ends
  • Spare machines pooled so a big job finishes in minutes
  • One price per site, not per person

Plan: Site licence per address

By trade

Trade price lists quoted correctly, tastings booked by text, the Sunday-hours question answered forty times a week without you.

Trades and services

Quotes built from your rates, job photos filed to the right client, the follow-up that otherwise never gets sent.

Clinics and practices

Notes tidied, letters drafted, reminders sent — and nothing about a patient uploaded anywhere, because the model is in the building.

Schools

Lesson material, reports and admin, with student work staying on the school network and no per-student fee.

Professional services

Contracts read, summaries written, precedent found — all under the confidentiality you already promised your clients.

Retail and hospitality

Rosters, stock lists, supplier emails and the website kept current, plus the phone answered while you are serving someone.
